# Competence nodes

An atom is one thing, explained once, for anyone who meets it. A working phase borrows
the atoms it needs; it does not re-explain them. Most atoms are already explained well
somewhere — in a tool's manual, in the Extended Matrix method — and then this page points
at them rather than writing them again, because a sentence that lives in two places ages
in two different ways.

What follows is the first list, drawn from what is written today. It will grow as the
phases are laid out, and it is deliberately shorter than the truth: an atom enters here
when somebody actually needs it to get through a phase.

## From the node's manual

These are explained in the manual of StratiGraph Server, in the part for whoever works.

**A room.** One study: one graph, the people who may enter, and what each may do.
**A role in a room** — owner, editor, viewer — and what each of the three may do.
**A profile on the node** — super, creator, user — which is a different question from the
role, and is asked separately.
**A person is an iD.** Why an ORCID, and what the node does with it.
**Signing in**, and what the node shows you before you have a room of your own.
**Sharing a room**: invitations, groups, and who ends up seeing the study.
**The catalogue**: what a node publishes of itself, and how that differs from a room.
**Publishing**: what changes, for you and for others, when a study becomes published.

## From the field

Explained in the manual of StratiField.

**A field node**: a node that travels, and what changes when it does.
**Working without a network**, and what happens when the network comes back.
**Recording a unit on site**, by voice or by hand, and signing what you said.

## From the method

Explained by the Extended Matrix method and taught by EM Academy. These are the atoms the
phases borrow most often, and the ones this Academy must never re-define on its own.

**A stratigraphic unit**, and the relations that order it.
**A virtual unit**, which is the reconstructive one, and what makes it a claim.
**A proxy**, and the difference between a proxy and a representation model.
**An epoch**, and what it means for a unit to survive into one.
**A source**, and what it means to say that a source holds up a claim.
**The level of detail** of a model, and what it promises to whoever reads it.

## What is missing, and said so

No atom yet covers **the chain from a recording to what was derived from it** — the audio
and its transcript, the photograph and the model made from it. The node does not write
those provenance stamps by itself today, so the atom would describe a habit rather than a
mechanism. It is the first one to write when it does.
