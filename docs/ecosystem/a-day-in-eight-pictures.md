# A day, in eight pictures

The eight steps of the backbone are the tools. This is the same thing from the other
side: **one day of work**, drawn as it happens, from the laboratory where the node is
packed to the screen where a child asks when the roof fell down. The drawings are the
ones used in teaching — they came before this page, and the Academy follows them rather
than inventing a sequence of its own.

Read them in order. Each caption says what the picture is about and, where it matters,
which gesture of the tools is being performed: that gesture has a page in a manual, and
the manual is one click away.

## 0 · Preparation

```{figure} /_static/comics/01-preparation.jpg
:alt: In the laboratory, two people load users, vocabularies and models onto a small node, and pack it into a rucksack.
:width: 100%

«Users, vocabularies and models on board» — «Then the node is ready to travel».
```

Before anybody leaves, the node is made ready: the people who may work in the room, the
vocabularies the discipline uses, the models the project starts from. A field node is a
small computer in a box, and it goes in the rucksack.
→ [the first day of a node](https://docs.stratigraph-eccch.eu/projects/node/en/latest/)

## 1 · Morning on site

```{figure} /_static/comics/02-morning-on-site.jpg
:alt: A clearing in the woods with cases, a generator and a field node; two people set up with their phones.
:width: 100%

«No internet up here… but StratiField sees yesterday's units».
```

The network is the first thing an excavation does not have. The field assistant does not
wait for it: the room is already on the node in the box, and yesterday's work is there
this morning.
→ [StratiField](https://docs.stratigraph-eccch.eu/projects/field/en/latest/)

## 1 · In the trench

```{figure} /_static/comics/03-in-the-trench.jpg
:alt: In the trench, an archaeologist dictates a stratigraphic unit into a phone while a colleague confirms on a tablet.
:width: 100%

«US 2041: collapse of tiles and mortar. Covers 2038».
```

A unit is recorded by speaking it, with the relation said in the same breath. The number
and the words are the archaeologist's; what the tool adds is that the sentence lands in
the graph, signed, instead of in a notebook to be copied later.

## 2 · Capturing in 3D

```{figure} /_static/comics/04-capturing-in-3d.jpg
:alt: Targets laid around a pile of collapsed tiles, a total station on the edge of the trench, a tablet showing the 3D box.
:width: 100%

«These are for the model. Targets in, positions measured».
```

The same collapse is measured so that it can be modelled, and the measurement is attached
to the unit rather than kept in a parallel archive of its own.

## 2 · The edge of the excavation

```{figure} /_static/comics/05-edge-of-the-excavation.jpg
:alt: Under a tent at the edge of the excavation, two people look at the first model on a laptop.
:width: 100%

«And the first *model* is already here» — «Relations fixed. I accept it into the study».
```

The model arrives while the trench is still open, and somebody **accepts it into the
study**: that acceptance is the gesture, not the rendering. Which phase this picture
belongs to is the one thing still to settle — it has a foot in measuring and a foot in
the work of the study.

## 3 · Evening: the data moves

```{figure} /_static/comics/06-evening-the-data-moves.jpg
:alt: Night. The node with a padlock; an antenna under a starry sky; a car boot with the equipment.
:width: 100%

«Safe on the node» — «Graph first, files when they can» — «Or back to base».
```

The day's work leaves the trench. What travels first is the graph, because it is small
and it is the argument; the heavy files follow when the line allows, or in the boot of a
car.
→ [from the field to the cloud](https://docs.stratigraph-eccch.eu/projects/node/en/latest/)

## 3 · The collaborative cloud

```{figure} /_static/comics/07-the-collaborative-cloud.jpg
:alt: A colleague in a city office looks at the morning's photographs on a wall screen and points at a sherd.
:width: 100%

«I can see this morning's photos. That *sherd* dates the collapse».
```

Somebody who was not on site reads this morning's work in the afternoon, and adds the one
thing that only they could see. This is what a shared room is for: not storage, but a
second pair of eyes with the right to speak.

## 4 · Dissemination

```{figure} /_static/comics/08-dissemination.jpg
:alt: A public in front of a large screen showing a reconstruction, with a QR code reading «Open data: scan to explore».
:width: 100%

«When did this roof fall down?» — and the data is open to whoever wants to look.
```

At the end the study is published, and the question a child asks in front of the screen
is answered by the same graph that was dictated into a phone in the trench.

## How the pictures map onto the phases

The headings above carry the **working phase** each picture belongs to, 0 to 4. Two
phases have two pictures, because they have two distinct moments; the fifth picture, the
edge of the excavation, is the one still to be placed, and it is marked as phase 2 here
provisionally.
